So What? 12 weeks since the Nth Cycle note · 6 weeks since Aepnus A

Nickel is 33% below where the Nth Cycle model puts it, and it broke on a policy reversal, not a cycle.
$16,796/t today (model: $25k). Down 14% in June alone. The cause is specific and durable: Indonesia is preparing to lift 2026 RKAB quotas back to ~360Mt from the 260Mt issued in H1, unwinding the exact tightening that drove the Q1 rally. Cobalt (+17%) and lithium (+78%) offset in the smaller lines. Copper at $13.34/kg clears the $13/kg the copper payback was underwritten at, so that platform holds.
Black mass payables printed all-time highs in Asia. That is the input side of the spread going the wrong way.
Fastmarkets assessed NCM CIF South Korea at 87.5% Ni/Co on 7 January — an all-time high — and high-purity powder meeting China's import spec printed above 100% payable for the first time. China domestic was 74–75% DDP in mid-June. Those are Asian benchmarks, not the EXW USA index Nth Cycle buys against, so this is a directional warning rather than a measured hit. But the direction is unambiguous and it compounds with the nickel gap.
Europe is already running the US export-ban experiment, and nobody is reading the results.
Fastmarkets describes a two-tier market: production scrap meeting China's spec commands premiums, while end-of-life material, particularly from Europe, faces weaker demand because of hazardous-waste export restrictions. That is precisely the mechanism the US EO is supposed to create — trapped feedstock, suppressed payable — running live in the jurisdiction where Rotterdam sits. If it holds, Rotterdam's spread should already be wider than South Carolina's, which the 26% SC / 19% NL base IRRs do not reflect.
No export ban. And the policy that did move went import-side, which is arguably better.
Nothing in the Federal Register, nothing from the White House, and the summer session has largely passed. So the 71% "most likely" SC case has not arrived. But the January 14 Section 232 proclamation on processed critical mineral imports reserved tariffs, contemplated minimum import prices, and required a negotiation report within 180 days — which fell on July 13, two days ago. A price floor on MHP would be worth more to Nth Cycle than most grant outcomes and is closer to landing than the ban.
Both rounds passed their target close dates without a public announcement.
Nth Cycle's Series C was to close June; PitchBook still shows ~$65.6M raised, consistent with no new round. Aepnus targeted a $10M first close by end of May. Neither is proof of failure — private rounds go unannounced — but on Nth Cycle's $8M cash and $15M/yr burn, straight-lining puts them near $4M today. Also live right now: the $100M DOE grant notification window, and the balance of Aepnus's demo grant expected "this summer."
Aepnus is untouched by all of the above. Nemaska is still the only thing that matters, and it is narrower than we thought.
The caustic spread ($950 Bécancour vs $214 production) has not moved. Rio Tinto has issued nothing since March 13. One correction worth having: the pilot that produced the 2,000-hour record never ran on Nemaska's feedstock — it used a nearby supplier's sodium sulfate carrying more impurities than the partners' own limits. The technical record is independent of Nemaska. The $36M/yr offtake is not.

What are we missing — the spread doesn't clear their own bar
Running the payability arithmetic at the model's own assumptions, before anything that happened this quarter: 82% out minus 65% in is 17 points. The number given to the National Energy Dominance Council was that the spread "has to be at least 20% for the economics to work." So the base case sits three points below the line the company itself gave the government. The CFO's "slightly better than breakeven" at 17% is consistent with that — it just means the base case is a breakeven case, not an economic one.
That reframes the export ban. It is not upside on a working model; it is the mechanism that closes a gap present in the base case. Which is why it is worth 25 IRR points, and why its absence matters more than a missed tailwind. Meanwhile every point of input payable eats the spread one-for-one, and at +1% output payable = $1.5M EBITDA, a five-point move in payables is ~$7.5M — roughly 20% of South Carolina's $38M EBITDA.
Is 17% the base case, or is 20% the base case and 17% the stress case? And what is the actual EXW USA payable being paid at Ohio today?
What are we missing — lithium is the widest-spread line and it's exposed twice
Lithium is bought at 0% payable and sold at 100%, and it is 42% of unit revenue ($16.4 of $39.3/kg). It is the most profitable line in the book precisely because none of its value is paid for on the way in — which means it is also the line most exposed to a price fall, since there is no input cost that falls with it. It is simultaneously the one process step never demonstrated at Ohio.
Those are filed as two separate risks. They are one risk on one line, and the +280kt LCE supply wave now points at it. If lithium retraces toward the $12k assumption, roughly 42% of unit revenue reprices with zero offsetting relief on feedstock cost.
Does FEL2 give us lithium recovery data, and does the model hold if lithium goes back to the "very conservative" $12k it was underwritten at?

Forward View thematic, not a recommendation

Copper is the exposure MUUS doesn't have, and Nth Cycle is already walking into it. The structural deficit into the 2030s is intact even through the June pullback, and it is the one major battery-adjacent metal where the portfolio has no direct position. What is new this quarter is that the cheapest route to that theme may already be owned: copper cleared $13.34/kg against the $13/kg the Nth Cycle copper add-on was underwritten at, so the $7M capex, sub-2-year payback, 70% incremental margin case holds at today's price rather than at a forecast. The Rio Tinto slag MTA signed in September 2025 runs twelve months from sample receipt, which puts a result around now, and commercial-scale copper cell testing is scheduled for Q2 2027. The 10x-LME powder premium that turns $4M of incremental revenue into $40M remains an assertion, not a validated price, and should stay out of any base case. The read: before sourcing new copper exposure, the question worth asking is what an accelerated copper platform inside the existing position would cost and return, since it arrives with no feedstock cost and no new counterparty.
